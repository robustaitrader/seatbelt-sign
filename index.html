What an alert looks like

How it works

  • Every hour, a model forecasts how much each coin is likely to move over the next 4 hours. It reads the coin's recent price swings, trading volume, funding rates and the time of day.
  • The sign comes on when a forecast is in the top tenth of that coin's last 30 days. A coin gets one post per episode: after an alert it stays quiet for 12 hours. At most 6 posts a day.
  • Every Monday a scorecard shows the live record since the channel started, misses included.

The evidence, and its limits

  • Tested on years the model never trained on (2023 to 2026): a big move (beyond 3 standard deviations within 4 hours) followed 7% of alert hours and 1.2% of the other hours, about 6 times as often.
  • So most alerts are not followed by a big move. An alert means "more likely", not "certain".
  • It can't tell direction, and nothing here says whether to buy or sell.
  • It has run live since 18 November 2026. Its live record is judged on 17 February 2027, by a rule written down before it started.
  • Not investment advice. Crypto prices can fall a long way quickly; never risk money you can't afford to lose.
